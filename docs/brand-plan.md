# Chef-bnb — Brand and Advertising Plan

2026-11-14 · Ernest

Source of truth is the Claude doc "Chef-bnb — Brand and Advertising Plan" (https://claude.ai/artifact/BmZDmsyLCaLuiP7G3zztrS); this file mirrors it so the plan lives with the code. When they drift, the doc wins; update this file.

## Summary

The brand is a warm, mostly neutral platform identity for underground dinner parties in NYC: polished enough that a stranger books a seat, loose enough that chefs like Casa Wong feel at home on it. This plan takes it from "working name and one accent color" to a do-it-yourself brand guide, a launch campaign and a $1,500 media plan for a late-December or January soft launch.

Five decisions that shape everything below:

1. **The platform is the stage, not the star.** Each chef's identity (Casa Wong's chili-red script, someone else's green) is the color. The platform's own system stays quiet: paper-warm neutrals, one accent, real photos of real tables.
2. **Guests first, solo and small group.** Voice, imagery and the first ads speak to a 25–38 year old deciding what to do on Thursday night. Chefs are recruited by hand with their own one-page pitch, not by ads.
3. **The vibe is late-night and a little undone, with a playful streak and serious plating.** Flash photos, candles, mismatched chairs; but the food is shot with care and the booking flow is as tight as Resy.
4. **Working name: Covers (decided Nov 14).** "Chef-bnb" read like a rental and invited an Airbnb trademark letter; Family Meal collided with Blue Hill. Covers is the working name pending the USPTO and handle checks in Naming.
5. **DIY identity, two pieces bought.** Ernest builds the system in Figma and the app. A designer is paid (or traded) for the wordmark and a small illustration set. The $1,000 ad credit goes to Instagram after the first dinners prove the creative.

## Brand foundations

The brand's job is trust at a glance: a stranger lands on an event page from an Instagram story and books a seat in a home they've never seen, cooked by a chef they've never met.

**Goal.** By the end of January: 8–10 dinners sold, 70%+ average fill, five chefs listed, and at least a third of seats booked by people who did not already follow Casa Wong. That last number is the brand working; the first three are the business working.

**Audience.** NYC, 25–38, already spends $60–150 on a night out, lives in or near Williamsburg / Greenpoint, LES / East Village, or Hell's Kitchen. Three jobs, in priority order:

1. **Solo, meet people.** Timeleft's customer: new to the city or newly single, wants a social night with low commitment and no small-talk apps.
2. **Small group night out.** 2–5 friends who are bored of restaurants and want a story to tell on Monday.
3. **Hosting an occasion.** Birthdays, team dinners, dates: buys out the table. Important revenue, but not the launch voice.

Chefs are the second audience: line cooks, sous chefs, culinary grads and existing supper-club hosts who want their own stage without DM chaos. They get a one-page pitch and personal outreach, not the public campaign.

**Positioning.** For New Yorkers who want a better night than another restaurant, Covers is where you book a seat at a chef's dinner party in a real home: ten seats at most, BYOB, cooked by a working chef. Resy books restaurants, Timeleft books strangers, Eatwith books tourists. We book the dinner party you wish you got invited to.

**Promise.** Every dinner on the platform is a real chef, a real home, ten people at most, bring your own bottle, address by email 48 hours before. You leave full and knowing someone new.

**Brand idea, one line.** "Dinner party, without the hosting." The platform does the inviting, the chef does the cooking, you just show up. Taglines to test alongside names:

- Pull up a chair.
- Ten seats. One chef. Bring a bottle.
- The best restaurant in New York is someone's apartment.
- Dinner's at a stranger's place.

**Personality.** The friend who always knows the spot: hospitable, candid, a little cheeky, unfussy, confident. Speaks like a host at the door, not a concierge or a brand.

Axes: raw/underground → polished (x), open to anyone → insider scene (y).

| Player | Position |
| --- | --- |
| NYC supper clubs on Instagram | Raw, insider |
| Casa Wong | Raw, scene |
| Dinner Lab (closed 2016) | Polished, scene |

| Luma | Polished, open |

The open space is the middle-left: the energy of the Instagram supper clubs with the "anyone can book" openness of Luma and Timeleft. Nobody there owns a brand; the supper clubs sell through link-in-bio and DMs.

### 2. Is / isn't

| The brand is | The brand isn't |
| --- | --- |
| A dinner party | A pop-up restaurant |
| Underground, but you're invited | Exclusive, gatekept, "members only" |
| Chef-driven | Chef-worship; no "culinary journey" language |
| A little undone: candles, mismatched chairs, flash photos | Sloppy: plating, timing and the booking flow are tight |
| For a Thursday night | Only for anniversaries |
| Specific NYC neighborhoods | "Cities worldwide" |
| Plain about the rules (BYOB, ten seats, address 48 h before) | Coy or legalistic |

## Naming

Decision (Nov 14): Covers is the working name. Family Meal was the first pick until the Blue Hill collision surfaced. Remaining checks before buying the domain: USPTO class 43, the Instagram handle, the say-it-aloud test. Runners-up if Covers fails a check: **Staff Meal** and **Ten Seats**.

**Why rename.** "Chef-bnb" says the category but reads like a rental listing, and Airbnb has opposed other "-bnb" marks; expect a letter once there's any press. Both chefbnb.com and chef-bnb.com are already registered by others.

**Criteria.** One or two words. Says dinner, seat, table, or is an insider term chefs will get. Works as an Instagram handle and in a sentence ("book it on ___", "I'm going to a ___ dinner"). No NYC restaurant or app already using it. A .com or .nyc available (.nyc needs a New York address).

| Name | Why it works | Risk | Domains (DNS checked Nov 14; "free" = no records, confirm at a registrar) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Family Meal** | The staff meal before service: chefs cooking for people they like. Warm, insider, underground | Live NYC collision: Dan Barber's Blue Hill in Greenwich Village now trades as "Family Meal at Blue Hill". Same city, same category; expect SEO and possibly legal friction | familymeal.nyc free; .com, .co, .club taken |
| **Staff Meal** | Same insider meaning as Family Meal (the crew's meal before service), without the Blue Hill collision. Slightly rougher, which fits | No NYC restaurant or app found under this name; still run the USPTO search | staffmeal.nyc free (registry confirms); .com and .co taken; .club free |
| **Covers** | Restaurant slang for seats served. One word, confident, sounds like a nightlife brand | Abstract to guests on first hearing; needs the tagline next to it | covers.co and covers.club free; covers.nyc and getcovers.com taken |
| **Ten Seats** | The product rule as the name: small, scarce, specific | Caps the brand if the rule ever changes | tenseats.nyc free; .com taken |
| **Saved Seat** | Casa Wong's "we saved you a spot", made the platform's promise | Softer, less distinctive | savedseat.nyc free; .com taken |

| House Plate | "House" = home, "plate" = chef | Flat; could be a dishware brand | houseplate.nyc free; .com taken |

**The one-week test.**

- [ ] Day 1: shortlist four names. Search each on USPTO (TESS, classes 43 and 35), Instagram, the App Store and Google's first page. Drop any with a live collision.
- [ ] Day 2: a three-question form to 15–20 target users from the Casa Wong waitlist and friends in the 25–38 bracket. Show each name beside the positioning line. Ask: which would you tap, what do you think it is, which sounds like it's for you.
- [ ] Day 2–4: an Instagram story poll on the Casa Wong account, two names at a time.
- [ ] Day 5: say-it-aloud test with three people: "Where were you last night?" "A ___ dinner." Keep the ones that don't need explaining.
- [ ] Day 6: buy the domain (.nyc at about $20 a year, plus the .com if it's under $50), register the Instagram and TikTok handles, set up `hello@`.

## Visual identity

Keep the system the app already has (Fraunces display, Geist UI, one terracotta accent, light and dark) and finish it: a wordmark, a neutral palette with names, photo rules, a small illustration set, and card-first layout. The platform looks like good paper; the chefs bring the color.

| Element | Direction | Casa Wong contrast |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Wordmark** | Lowercase serif wordmark in Fraunces (or a licensed cut later), no symbol at launch. One drawn gesture may attach to it (a chair, a bell) once the name is chosen. Commission this from a designer; it is the one piece worth paying for | Casa Wong is a hand script; the platform is typeset. Never a script |
| **Palette** | Five tokens: paper `#f5f1e9`, ink `#1c1917`, muted `#6b655c`, accent terracotta `#c2410c` (already in the app; `#fb923c` in dark mode), night `#17150f` for dark surfaces. One accent only; chef pages may add the chef's own color in their header | Casa Wong's chili `#81271f` and paste `#a84334` stay theirs. Terracotta is warm and adjacent without being red |
| **Type** | Fraunces for headlines (use its optical size and "soft" axis for the playful streak), Geist for UI and body, Geist Mono for metadata: dates, neighborhoods, "3 of 8 seats", prices. Mono metadata is the one visible borrow | Casa Wong uses Canela and Menlo. The Canela file in the resources folder is a trial license; don't ship it |
| **Photography** | Real tables, real people, mid-moment. Flash or candlelight, never studio. Hero image = the table and the people; the plate is the second image, shot with care. Vertical 4:5 for Instagram, 3:2 for event cards. No stock, no flat lays with props, no overhead "content" shots | Same documentary style as the brand guide shoot. Reuse Casa Wong's library for launch, credited |
| **Illustration** | 8–12 single-line ink drawings: chair, bottle, candle, doorbell, keys, stacked plates, a table for ten, a "10". Used in empty states, email headers, stickers. Drawn in ink, never in the accent color | Casa Wong's red noodles and candles stay theirs; same medium, different subjects and color |
| **Texture** | A faint paper grain (3–4% opacity) on hero and email backgrounds only, never on UI surfaces | The "little bit" of Casa Wong |
| **Layout** | Event card first: photo, chef name, neighborhood in mono, date, price per seat, seats left. Hairline borders, no shadows, generous whitespace, 8 px grid. Buttons are filled accent with the serif off | Luma's event-page clarity with warmer materials |
| **Dark mode** | Keep it. People book at night on their phones. Paper becomes night, terracotta lightens | Casa Wong's green and red fields are the reference for how dark can still feel warm |

**Three rules that keep it coherent.**

1. One accent on any screen or post. If a chef's color is present, the platform accent steps back to ink.
2. Photos carry the mood; type carries the information. Headlines never shout over a photo.
3. Metadata is always mono. If it's a date, a place, a count or a price, it's in Geist Mono.

## Voice and copy

The voice is a host at the door: second person, present tense, short sentences, one joke per screen at most. The current home-page copy already has it ("Dinner parties, cooked by real chefs, in real homes."); the job is to hold that line everywhere, including emails and error states.

**Five rules.**

1. State the rule, then the reason, in one breath. "BYOB. Chefs can't sell drinks at home, so bring something you like."
2. Specifics over adjectives. The dish, the neighborhood, the number of seats. Never "amazing".
3. Cheeky once per screen, never in payment, cancellation or error copy.
4. Chefs are people with names, not "culinary talent". Guests are "you", never "diners" or "foodies".
5. Banned words: curated, culinary journey, elevated, bespoke, foodie, experience (as a noun), unlock, discover, community (as a noun).

**Headline bank** (for ads, stories, the home page; test the top three in the first ad set):

- Dinner's at a stranger's place.
- Pull up a chair.
- Ten seats. One chef. Bring a bottle.
- Come alone. Leave with eight new numbers.
- Cooked by a chef, in a kitchen that isn't yours.
- The best restaurant in New York is someone's apartment.
- Better than a restaurant, easier than hosting.
- You're on the list.

**Microcopy for key moments.** Proposed changes to the app; keep whatever already matches.

| Moment | Now | Proposed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home hero | Dinner parties, cooked by real chefs, in real homes. | Keep |
| Event card, seats | 3 seats left | `3 seats left · 2 going solo` (mono) |
| Request button | Request seats | Save my seat / Hold 2 seats |
| After requesting | Booking requested | You're on the list. [Chef] confirms your seat once your payment lands, usually the same day. |
| Seat confirmed | Confirmed | You're in. Address arrives by email two days before. |
| Address email subject | — | Here's the address for [Thursday]. |
| Review request subject | — | How was dinner at [Chef]'s? |
| Empty calendar | No dinners on the calendar yet | Nothing this week. Next dinners drop Monday; follow [handle] to hear first. |
| Request expired | Expired | Your hold ran out. The seats went back on the table; grab them again if they're still there. |
| Chef pitch | Cook for a table of ten. | Keep |
| Sign-in | Sign in | Come in |

**Email voice.** Subject lines of five words or fewer. Sent from the brand name with a real first name in the signature. One action per email, as a button, in the accent color. Plain text wins over design; the paper texture and one line drawing in the header are the whole decoration.

**Chef voice on the platform.** Chefs write their own bios and menus in their own voice; the platform never rewrites them. The intake form asks three prompts instead of "bio": what you cook, where you learned it, what a night at your table is like.

## Advertising plan

Organic and chef-led channels fill the first dinners; the $1,000 ad credit is spent only after three real dinners have produced real photos and a measured request rate. Paid traffic to an empty calendar is wasted.

**Objectives to January 31.**

- 8–10 dinners listed and 70%+ of seats filled
- 500 emails on the weekly drop list, 1,000 Instagram followers on the platform account
- A third of seats booked by people outside the Casa Wong orbit
- Cost per seat request under $15 on paid, once paid runs

**Channels, in the order to switch them on.**

| # | Channel | What runs | Cost | Expected share of early seats |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Chefs' own Instagram | Each chef posts the dinner with the booking link; the platform reposts and tags | $0 | Largest |
| 2 | Casa Wong account and waitlist | Launch announcement, name polls, first-dinner stories | $0 | Large |
| 3 | Platform Instagram and TikTok | Three posts a week: a dinner drop, a chef, a recap from inside the dinner | Time | Medium, grows |
| 4 | Weekly drop email | "This week's tables" every Monday morning to the list, through Resend | $0 | Medium; best repeat channel |
| 5 | Partnerships | One dinner per partner with four reserved seats: a Williamsburg run club, a climbing gym, one workplace ERG | A seat or two | Medium |
| 6 | Paid Instagram | Two ad sets, three creatives each, 25–38, Brooklyn and Manhattan, interests Resy, Timeleft, Eatwith, supper club, cooking | $1,000 credit | Medium; the scalable one |
| 7 | Earned | Pitch Nonsense NYC, Time Out, The Infatuation and two NYC newsletters after five dinners, with the "Resy for supper clubs" story | $0 | Spiky |
| 8 | Street | Table cards at dinners, stickers at cafes and gyms in the two neighborhood clusters | $60 | Small, but on-brand |

**Paid, in detail.** Assumes the credit is Meta; if it's Google, run search on "supper club NYC" and "dinner party NYC" instead and skip display. Install the Meta pixel with a `request_seats` event before the first dollar. Pacing: nothing in November; $300 in December across three weeks to test creatives; $700 in January at about $175 a week behind the weekly drop. Rough NYC numbers for a lifestyle audience: $12–20 CPM and about 1% click-through, so the credit buys roughly 60–80k impressions and 600–800 visits; at a 3–5% request rate that is 20–35 seat requests. These are estimates to beat, not benchmarks.

**Three campaign concepts.** Shoot all three at the first dinners; keep the one with the best click-through.

1. **Dinner's at a stranger's place.** An apartment door, a buzzer, a mono slip reading "address arrives 48 h before". Speaks to the solo job. Story and 4:5 post.
2. **Ten seats.** A table with chairs filling in frame by frame, "3 left" in mono. Urgency for the group job. Works as a 6-second video.
3. **[Chef] is cooking Thursday.** A chef portrait at their stove, the menu in three lines, eight seats. Chef-led and repeatable for every dinner; also the format chefs post themselves.

**Weekly content rhythm.**

| Day | What goes out |
| --- | --- |
| Monday | Drop email and post: this week's tables, seats left per dinner |
| Wednesday | Chef story: behind the stove, prep, the shopping |
| Dinner nights | Flash-photo stories from the table, posted the same night, reposted by the chef |
| Sunday | "Seats left" reminder story for the week ahead |

**Partnership offer.** Not a discount (pricing is the chef's). Offer first pick: the partner's members see the dinner 24 hours before the public drop and get four seats held together. One dinner per partner in January; the partner posts once.

## Measurement

Nine numbers, reviewed every Monday for 30 minutes before the drop email goes out.

| Metric | Target by Jan 31 | Where it's measured |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fill rate per dinner (confirmed seats / max) | 70% | events + bookings; add a column to the admin event list |
| Seat requests per week | 20 by mid-January | booking_log |
| Request to paid within 48 h | 80% | booking_log |
| Seats from outside the Casa Wong orbit | 33% | "How did you hear about this?" dropdown on the request form, plus UTM source stored on the booking |
| Repeat guests within 60 days | 15% | bookings per user |
| Weekly drop list | 500 emails, 45% open rate | Resend |
| Platform Instagram | 1,000 followers; story link taps per drop | Instagram insights |
| Paid | cost per seat request under $15, click-through over 1% | Meta Ads Manager with the pixel's `request_seats` event |
| Review average | 4.6 or better | reviews |

**Instrumentation to add before launch** (one small PR): capture `utm_source` on first visit and write it to the booking; a one-question source dropdown on the request form (chef's Instagram, friend, Casa Wong, the platform's Instagram, ad, partner, other); the Meta pixel with a `request_seats` event; and cookie-free page analytics (Vercel Web Analytics is on the free tier). Keep the dashboard to a SQL view on the admin page.

**The Monday review.** Last week's fill per dinner, requests by source, the best-performing creative, and one change for the week. If a dinner is under 50% four days out, it gets the Sunday story and a partner nudge; if it's still under minimum at the 72-hour cutoff, the go/no-go job cancels it and the chef gets a note, not a surprise.
